## Details

- Registered on 24 April 1925 — the first Islamic congregation in Finland
- Official names: Suomen Islam-seurakunta, Finlands islamska församling, Finlandiya İslam Cemaati
- 433 members according to official records for 2024
- Membership is open to Tatars only and requires knowledge of the Tatar language
- Prayer rooms not only in Helsinki, but also in Turku, Järvenpää and Kotka
- Publishes its own magazine, "Mähällä Habärläre"

## About

The congregation's official name is Suomen Islam-seurakunta (the Finnish Islamic Congregation); the name "Islamiya" became attached to it in Russian-language sources. It was registered on 24 April 1925 as Suomen muhamettilainen seurakunta and became the first Islamic congregation in Finland; it has carried its present name since 1963. Official records for 2024 list 433 members.

In 1960 Finnish Tatars used their own funds to build a five-storey house on Fredrikinkatu in Helsinki, which houses the cultural centre, the prayer hall, a Sunday school, a kindergarten, the "Yolduz" sports club, a library, a concert hall and a canteen. In the 1970s the "Islamiya" congregation, led by the furrier Osman Ali, merged with the G. Tukay Society, led by Professor Gumar Daher, into a single diaspora.

The supreme body is the congregation's assembly, which every three years elects the chair and the board; the staff includes an imam, muezzins, a teacher and a clerk, and seven committees are at work, among them a committee for the native language and a committee for cemeteries. The congregation's prayer rooms are used by other Muslims as well, and the congregation does no missionary work. Attached to it is the FTB association (Finlandiya Türkleri Birliği / Suomen Turkkilaisten Seura), founded in 1935.
