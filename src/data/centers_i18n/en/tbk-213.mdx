## About

The Tatar Cultural Society of Latvia "Idel" was founded in Riga in 1988 — one of the oldest in the Baltic states. Its creation was announced by the newspaper Padomju Jaunatne on 3 December 1988; in 1993 the society was re-registered as Latvijas Tatāru biedrība "Ideļ" under number 40008002584, and the state register lists it as active. According to the TATARICA encyclopaedia, Z. L. Zainullin and I. S. Sabitov stood at the society's origins; Ramil Kalinkin, Musavir Sharipov, Haris Fakhrazeev and the Galikhanov and Salyakhov brothers are also named among the founders.

The society opened a Sunday school of the Tatar language — running since 1991 — assembled a library and holds concerts by performers from Tatarstan and Bashkortostan; for more than twenty years Latvian state radio has aired a Tatar programme presented by the journalist Rinats Ahunovs. On the initiative of "Idel", Riga hosts a Sabantuy attended by Tatars from Latvia, Lithuania and Estonia.

The society belongs to the Ita Kozakeviča Association of National Cultural Societies of Latvia and works from its house of societies at Slokas iela 37; as of November 2024 its chair, Rinats Ahunovs, sits on the association's board. The society has no website or social media of its own.

"Idel" and the Tatar-Bashkir society "Chishme" are separate organisations, not one under two names: they have different registration numbers, different chairs and twelve years between them. The connection between them is familial: "Idel" was founded by the writer Zaki Zainullin, while "Chishme" was created in 2000 by his son Zufar Zainullin. Religious bodies share the same name in the register — the Muslim congregations "Ideļ" in Riga (1994) and Daugavpils (1995) — and these are separate legal entities.
