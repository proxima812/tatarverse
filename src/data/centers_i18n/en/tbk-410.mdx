## About

Turun Turkkilais-Tataarilainen Yhdistys was founded in the late 1930s - in 1937 or 1938, according to different sources - a few years after the similar societies in Helsinki and Tampere, both established in 1935. Since 1951 the organisation has been known as Turun turkkilainen yhdistys. Like its sister societies, it taught the Tatar language and ran cultural and publishing activities.

Turku is one of the four cities where Finnish Tatars have traditionally lived, alongside Helsinki, Tampere and Järvenpää; the community originally grew up around Tatar textile traders. The Finnish Islamic Congregation (Suomen Islam-seurakunta, founded in Helsinki in 1925 as the country's first Islamic congregation) is also active in Turku: it keeps a prayer room at Linnankatu 9-11, and the city has a Tatar Muslim cemetery - one of three in the country, along with those in Helsinki and Tampere.

Unlike the Tatars of Tampere, the Tatars of Turku did not establish a separate religious congregation and confined themselves to a cultural society. That also explains why the organisation has no website, no social media and no entry in the registers of associations available to us: its current activity cannot be confirmed from open sources.
