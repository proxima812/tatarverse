## About

The Tatar community of Narva is the oldest in Estonia: Tatar traders settled here in the 1860s. In 1928 Veliulla Fetkullin founded the Narva Tatar Cultural Society; the religious association Narva Muhamedi Kogudus was registered in the city the same year, and on 29 July 1935 Estonia's first mosque opened. The present legal entity, MTÜ Narva Tatari Kultuuriselts, was registered on 4 March 1998; its main field of activity under the EMTAK classification is associations and cultural societies of national minorities.

In 2012 the Tatars of Narva were the principal organisers of the 8th Baltic Sabantuy. The society runs the festival "Folklore in Tatar Song and Dance" and the international Tatar festival "Balti Sabantui". For many years the society was led by Marjam Malysheva; since February 2022 Rafael Sharafetdinov has been listed as a board member, and he focuses on work with young people. The same Rafael Sharafetdinov also heads the Tatar vocal studio "ALTÕN" in Tallinn.

The society belongs to the umbrella organisation Rahvusvaheline Rahvuskultuuride Ühenduste Liit "Lüüra". According to the census, about 75 residents of Narva name Tatar as their mother tongue.
